--- A. Valiant's VP vs VNP, permanent vs determinant ---

A1
- Claim: Mignon and Ressayre (2004) proved that the determinantal complexity of the n x n permanent is at least n^2/2 (over characteristic zero); search results consistently describe this quadratic bound as still the best known lower bound for the permanent, against Grenet's upper bound of 2^n - 1.
- Source: T. Mignon, N. Ressayre, "A quadratic bound for the determinant and permanent problem", IMRN 2004 (journal page not located); supporting search hits: Macaulay2 Permanents package docs (https://www.macaulay2.com/doc/Macaulay2/share/doc/Macaulay2/Permanents/html/index.html), Ikenmeyer and Landsberg arXiv 1610.00159 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1610.00159), arXiv 2402.17839 (https://arxiv.org/html/2402.17839v2).
- Quote: none verbatim fetched; search-result summary: "Ikenmeyer and Landsberg's 2016 arXiv paper lists the known bounds as m²/2 below and 2^m − 1 above."
- Status: unverified (search snippet only).

A2
- Claim: No lower bound on determinantal complexity that is superlinear in max{number of variables, degree} is known for ANY explicit polynomial (note: n^2/2 for perm_n is linear in its n^2 variables). Kumar and Volk (2020) prove dc(sum_i x_i^n) >= 1.5n - 3, the first explicit bound exceeding the trivial degree bound by a constant factor.
- Source: Mrinal Kumar, Ben Lee Volk, "A Lower Bound on Determinantal Complexity", arXiv:2009.02452 (CCC 2021 per memory). Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.02452
- Quote: "it is a long standing open problem to prove a lower bound which is super linear in max{n,d}." and "We prove that the determinantal complexity of the polynomial sum_{i=1}^n x_i^n is at least 1.5n - 3."
- Status: verified (fetched).

A3
- Claim: 2024 to 2026 activity on the permanent bound is incremental: Bedi (ECCC TR24-015, Jan 2024) gives an elementary proof of a quadratic lower bound for dc(perm) in positive characteristic (Cai, Chen, Li had earlier extended Mignon-Ressayre to characteristic other than 2, Computational Complexity 2010, per search snippet). The closest 2026 item, ECCC TR26-097 (Sheshadri, June 2026), proves an asymptotic n^2/(2e) symmetric determinantal lower bound for diagonal power sums, not for the permanent. No 2025 or 2026 improvement of n^2/2 for the permanent was found.
- Source: Harpreet Bedi, "Degree 2 lower bound for Permanent in arbitrary characteristic", ECCC TR24-015. Fetched: https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2024/015/ ; TR26-097 only via search (https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/097/).
- Quote: "An elementary proof of quadratic lower bound for determinantal complexity of the permanent" "in positive characteristic is stated."
- Status: verified (fetched) for Bedi 2024; Cai-Chen-Li and TR26-097 parts: unverified (search snippet only).

--- B. Geometric complexity theory ---

B1
- Claim: Mulmuley and Sohoni (2001) proposed strengthening the permanent vs determinant conjecture over C to separating the GL_{n^2}(C)-orbit closures of the determinant and the padded permanent, to be certified by representation-theoretic "occurrence obstructions" (irreducible representations occurring in one coordinate ring but not the other).
- Source: As stated in the abstract of Bürgisser, Ikenmeyer, Panova, "No occurrence obstructions in geometric complexity theory", arXiv:1604.06431, fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.06431 . Venue of the original (K. Mulmuley, M. Sohoni, "Geometric complexity theory I", SIAM J. Comput. 31(2), 2001): SIAM page fetch failed (HTTP 403); a search snippet (Gesmundo, Ikenmeyer, Panova, arXiv 1611.00827) cites "SIAM J. Comput. papers from 2001 and 2008".
- Quote: "suggested to study a strengthened version of this conjecture over the complex numbers" ... "separating the orbit closures of the determinant and padded permanent polynomials." ... "which are irreducible representations of GL_{n^2}(C)"
- Status: verified (fetched) for the content as restated by BIP; venue details unverified (search snippet only).

B2
- Claim: Bürgisser, Ikenmeyer, Panova (FOCS 2016; J. AMS 2019) proved that occurrence obstructions cannot separate the determinant and padded-permanent orbit closures, i.e. the original Mulmuley-Sohoni occurrence-obstruction program is impossible; they explicitly do not rule out multiplicity obstructions. The permanent vs determinant problem is equivalent to VP_ws vs VNP.
- Source: Peter Bürgisser, Christian Ikenmeyer, Greta Panova, "No occurrence obstructions in geometric complexity theory", arXiv:1604.06431 (comments line: "To appear in the Journal of the AMS"). Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.06431
- Quote: "We prove that this approach is impossible." ... "we do not rule out the general approach to the permanent versus determinant problem via multiplicity obstructions." ... "is equivalent to the separation of the complexity classes VP_{ws} and VNP."
- Status: verified (fetched) (FOCS 2016 venue: unverified (memory); JAMS: page says "To appear in the Journal of the AMS").

B3
- Claim: Ikenmeyer and Panova (FOCS 2016; Advances in Mathematics 319 (2017) 40-66) showed that vanishing of rectangular Kronecker coefficients cannot prove superpolynomial determinantal complexity lower bounds for the permanent (the precursor to B2).
- Source: C. Ikenmeyer, G. Panova, "Rectangular Kronecker coefficients and plethysms in geometric complexity theory", arXiv:1512.03798 (https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1512.03798), not fetched.
- Quote (from search snippet, not a fetched page): "We prove that in the geometric complexity theory program the vanishing of rectangular Kronecker coefficients cannot be used to prove superpolynomial determinantal complexity lower bounds for the permanent polynomial."
- Status: unverified (search snippet only).

B4
- Claim: What GCT can still hope to do: multiplicity obstructions. Dörfler, Ikenmeyer, Panova (2019) show multiplicity obstructions are strictly stronger than occurrence obstructions; Ikenmeyer and Kandasamy (STOC 2020, pp. 713-726; arXiv 1911.03990) give the first implementation of the Mulmuley-Sohoni approach, separating the orbit closures of the power-sum polynomial and the product of variables using only symmetry information and representation-theoretic multiplicities, with an obstruction that is neither an occurrence obstruction nor a vanishing-ideal occurrence obstruction.
- Source: Dörfler, Ikenmeyer, Panova, arXiv 1901.04576 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.04576.pdf); Ikenmeyer, Kandasamy, "Implementing geometric complexity theory: On the separation of orbit closures via symmetries", STOC 2020 (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.03990). Neither fetched.
- Quote (search snippet, DIP 2019): "Multiplicity obstructions are stronger than occurrence obstructions... The existence of such strong occurrence obstructions has been recently disproven in 2016 in two successive papers"
- Status: unverified (search snippet only).

B5
- Claim: Bläser, Ikenmeyer, Lysikov, Pandey, Schreyer (2019; FOCS 2020 per memory) prove NP-hardness of orbit closure containment for 3-tensors, characterize generating tensors almost by their symmetries, prove nontrivial equations via GCT methods, and read this as a positive sign for GCT.

--- F. Extension complexity (a closed route) ---

F1
- Claim: Fiorini, Massar, Pokutta, Tiwary, de Wolf (STOC 2012; JACM 62(2), 2015) solved Yannakakis's 20-year-old problem: there is no polynomial-size LP whose polytope projects to the TSP polytope, even without symmetry; likewise for the cut and stable set polytopes.
- Source: Samuel Fiorini, Serge Massar, Sebastian Pokutta, Hans Raj Tiwary, Ronald de Wolf, "Exponential Lower Bounds for Polytopes in Combinatorial Optimization", arXiv:1111.0837 (comments: "This version of the paper will appear in the Journal of the ACM"). Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.0837
- Quote: "there exists no polynomial-size linear program (LP) whose associated polytope projects to the traveling salesman polytope"
- Status: verified (fetched).

F2
- Claim: Rothvoss (STOC 2014; JACM 64(6), 2017): the perfect matching polytope of K_n has extension complexity 2^{Omega(n)}, answering the central open question of the field negatively, and raising the TSP polytope bound to 2^{Omega(n)}. Since perfect matching is in P, this shows extension complexity lower bounds do not track P vs NP.
- Source: Thomas Rothvoss, "The matching polytope has exponential extension complexity", arXiv:1311.2369 (v4, 2017). Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.2369
- Quote: "the central question in this field remained wide open" "We answer this question negatively." "the extension complexity of the perfect matching polytope in a complete n-node graph is 2^Omega(n)"
- Status: verified (fetched) (JACM 2017 venue: unverified (memory)).

F3
- Claim: Lee, Raghavendra, Steurer (STOC 2015): the cut, TSP and stable set polytopes are not linear images of any spectrahedron of dimension below 2^{n^c}; first super-polynomial lower bounds on semidefinite extension complexity; polynomial-size SDPs are no stronger than low-degree sum-of-squares for max-CSPs.
- Source: James R. Lee, Prasad Raghavendra, David Steurer, "Lower bounds on the size of semidefinite programming relaxations", arXiv:1411.6317. Fetched: https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.6317

- Status: verified (fetched) (STOC 2015 venue: unverified (memory)).

I2

I3

"lower bound techniques against weak circuit models quite often easily extend" ... "This explains why direct adaptations of certain lower bounds are unlikely to yield strong complexity separations"
- Status: verified (fetched) (ITCS 2020 / JACM 2022 venue: unverified (memory)).

(III) RECENT STRUCTURAL RESULTS (TIME-SPACE)
==================================================

L1
- Claim: Cook and Mertz (STOC 2024; ECCC TR23-174, Nov 2023): Tree Evaluation is in space O(log n * log log n), improving their 2020 bound of O(log^2 n / log log n); TreeEval was the central candidate for separating P from L via composition, and the result greatly strengthens the case that it is in L, leaving its status as a hard problem for L "a mystery"; the technique uses catalytic ("amortized") branching programs. Goldreich (ECCC TR24-124, Dec 2024) later shaved a Theta(log log(h+l)) factor in the regime h >= l (search snippet), which does not apply to the regime used by Williams.
- Source: James Cook, Ian Mertz, "Tree Evaluation is in Space O(log n · log log n)", ECCC TR23-174. Fetched: https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2023/174/ ; Goldreich: https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2024/124 (not fetched).
- Quote: "Our main result is to improve the space complexity of TreeEval to O(log n · log log n)" ... "a central candidate for separating polynomial time (P) from logarithmic space (L) via composition." ... "Thus its status as a candidate hard problem for L remains a mystery." ... "amortized branching programs, also known as catalytic branching programs"
- Status: verified (fetched) (STOC 2024 venue: unverified (search snippet only); Goldreich part: unverified (search snippet only)).

L2
- Claim: Williams (STOC 2025; ECCC TR25-017, Feb 2025; arXiv 2502.17779; JACM 2026 per a secondary page): every multitape Turing machine running in time t >= n can be simulated in space O(sqrt(t log t)), i.e. TIME[t] is contained in SPACE[O(sqrt(t log t))], improving Hopcroft, Paul, Valiant's O(t/log t) of 1975. Consequences: bounded fan-in circuits of size s can be evaluated in sqrt(s)*poly(log s) space; there are explicit problems solvable in O(n) space that require n^{2-eps} time on multitape TMs; the author describes this as "a little progress" on P vs PSPACE. The key tool is the Cook-Mertz Tree Evaluation algorithm applied to implicitly defined instances.
- Source: Ryan Williams, "Simulating Time in Square-Root Space", ECCC TR25-017 (24 Feb 2025). Fetched: https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2025/017/ ; also fetched arXiv:2502.17779 "Simulating Time With Square-Root Space" (comments: "To appear in ACM STOC 2025"): https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17779
- Quote: "every multitape Turing machine running in time t can be simulated in space only O(sqrt(t log t))" ... "bounded fan-in circuits of size s can be evaluated on any input in only sqrt(s) · poly(log s) space" ... "explicit problems solvable in O(n) space which require n^{2-epsilon} time on a multitape Turing machine" ... "thereby making a little progress on the P versus PSPACE problem." ... "leveraging the remarkable space-efficient algorithm for Tree Evaluation recently found by Cook and Mertz"
- Status: verified (fetched).

WHAT IS OPEN (with the page that says so)
==================================================
1. Permanent vs determinant / VP_ws vs VNP: open. Best dc lower bound still quadratic (A1, snippet); "it is a long standing open problem to prove a lower bound which is super linear in max{n,d}" (Kumar-Volk, fetched, A2). GCT route: occurrence obstructions are dead ("We prove that this approach is impossible", B2), multiplicity obstructions are not ruled out ("we do not rule out the general approach ... via multiplicity obstructions", B2).
2. Superpolynomial lower bounds for general (unbounded-depth) algebraic circuits: open (unverified (memory): Baur-Strassen Omega(n log d) remains the best; no fetched page states this). Constant depth is solved over all fields (C1, C2).
3. Is MCSP NP-hard: open; "It has been an open question since Levin's seminal work on NP-completeness whether MCSP is NP-complete." (Ilango, fetched, G5/G9); known only for partial MCSP (G4) and with a random oracle (G5).
4. Tree Evaluation in L: open; "its status as a candidate hard problem for L remains a mystery" (Cook-Mertz, fetched, L1).
5. P vs PSPACE: open; Williams's theorem is "a little progress on the P versus PSPACE problem" (fetched, L2). Improving sqrt(t log t) to sqrt(t): open per the tracker page (secondary, L4).
6. Near-maximum circuit lower bounds for classes below S_2E (e.g. for NP, Delta_2E, ZPE^NP without advice): no 2025 or 2026 result found (H5, search only); the frontier is S_2E a.e. (H4, fetched) and E^{prMA}/1 (H5).
7. Magnification: the locality barrier (I3, fetched) explains why no known weak-model lower bound has triggered magnification; no 2025 or 2026 breakthrough found.
