=== A. FRAMEWORK ===

A1
- Claim: Cook and Reckhow (JSL 1979) defined propositional proof systems and p-simulation, and connected proof size to NP vs coNP: if every tautology (equivalently every unsatisfiable formula) has a polynomial-size proof in some proof system then NP = coNP; the full statement is the equivalence NP = coNP iff a polynomially bounded proof system exists.
- Source: (i) Yaroslav Alekseev, "A Lower Bound for Polynomial Calculus with Extension Rule", Theory of Computing, Volume 22(4), 2026, pp. 1-29, Special Issue CCC 2021, DOI 10.4086/toc.2026.v022a004; fetched https://theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v022a004/v022a004.pdf. (ii) [BN21] definitions section and reference list, entry as printed: "Stephen A. Cook and Robert A. Reckhow. The relative efficiency of propositional proof systems. Journal of Symbolic Logic, 44(1):36-50, March 1979. Preliminary version in STOC '74."
- Quote: Alekseev [PDF text layer]: "In the same work Cook and Rechow introduced a notion of p-simulation: a proof system Π p-simulates a proof system Ψ if every tautology has a proof in Π of size at most polynomially larger than in its shortest proof in Ψ" and "has a short proof of unsatisfiability, then NP = coNP". [BN21] [PDF text layer]: "We say that P' polynomially simulates P if, for all x ∈ L, the size of the shortest P'-proof of x is polynomially bounded by the size of the shortest P-proof of x."
- Status: verified (fetched) for the citation, the p-simulation definition, and the direction "polynomially bounded proof system implies NP = coNP". The converse direction of the "iff" was not read on any fetched page: unverified (memory). (The Cambridge Core JSL page https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-symbolic-logic/article/abs/relative-efficiency-of-propositional-proof-systems/218048250981F835B4B2A4080205A0BA was located by search but not fetched.)

=== B. RESOLUTION ===

B1
- Claim: Haken (1985): the pigeonhole principle PHP^{n+1}_n (clauses "every pigeon gets a hole" and "no hole gets two pigeons") requires resolution refutation length exp(Ω(n)); the proof extends to the onto functional variant.
- Source: [BN21], Section 7.4.1; entry as printed: "Armin Haken. The intractability of resolution. Theoretical Computer Science, 39(2-3):297-308, August 1985." Fetched URL: https://www.jakobnordstrom.se/docs/publications/ProofComplexityChapter.pdf
- Quote [PDF text layer]: "require length exp(Ω(n)) in resolution for m = n + 1 pigeons, and his proof can be extended to work also for the onto FPHP formulas"
- Status: verified (fetched)

B2
- Claim: Urquhart (1987): Tseitin formulas on expander graphs require resolution length exp(Ω(N)) (N = formula size), the first truly exponential resolution lower bound in terms of formula size; Chvátal and Szemerédi (1988): random k-CNF formulas with a large enough constant clause density require resolution length exp(Ω(N)) asymptotically almost surely.
- Source: [BN21], Section 7.4.1; entries as printed: "Alasdair Urquhart. Hard examples for resolution. Journal of the ACM, 34(1):209-219, January 1987." and "Vašek Chvátal and Endre Szemerédi. Many hard examples for resolution. Journal of the ACM, 35(4):759-768, October 1988." Fetched URL as in B1.
- Quote [PDF text layer]: "The first truly exponential lower bound on length was obtained for Tseitin formulas"; "exp(Ω(N)) if the underlying graph is a well-connected so-called expander graph"; "exp(Ω(N)) to refute such formulas (again asymptotically almost surely)".
- Status: verified (fetched)

B3
- Claim: Ben-Sasson and Wigderson (JACM 2001) size-width relation: for a k-CNF over n variables, resolution refutation length is at least exp(Ω((refutation width - k)^2 / n)), so width lower bounds imply length lower bounds.
- Source: [BN21], Section 7.4.2, inequality (7.9); entry as printed: "Eli Ben-Sasson and Avi Wigderson. Short proofs are narrow- resolution made simple. Journal of the ACM, 48(2):149-169, March 2001. Preliminary version in STOC '99." Fetched URL as in B1.
- Quote [PDF text layer]: "strong enough width lower bounds imply strong length lower bounds" and "(7.9) where "refutation length" and "refutation width" mean the minimum length and minimum width, respectively, of any resolution refutations of the formula."
- Status: verified (fetched)

B4
- Claim: CDCL with nondeterministic decisions, unlimited clause retention, an asserting learning scheme (e.g. 1UIP) and restarts polynomially simulates resolution (Pipatsrisawat and Darwiche, AIJ 2011; Atserias, Fichte and Thurley, JAIR 2011); the survey stresses this is a result about nondeterministic solvers.
- Source: [BN21], Section 7.3; entries as printed: "Knot Pipatsrisawat and Adnan Darwiche. On the power of clause-learning SAT solvers as resolution engines. Artificial Intelligence, 175(2):512-525, February 2011. Preliminary version in CP '09." and "Albert Atserias, Johannes Klaus Fichte, and Marc Thurley. Clause-learning algorithms with many restarts and bounded-width resolution. Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research, 40:353-373, January 2011. Preliminary version in SAT '09." Fetched URL as in B1.
- Quote [PDF text layer]: "shown that nondeterministic CDCL can be as efficient as the resolution proof system except possibly for a polynomial blow-up."
- Status: verified (fetched)

=== C. CUTTING PLANES ===

C1
- Claim: Pudlák (JSL 1997) proved exponential cutting planes lower bounds for clique-colouring formulas via a Craig interpolation argument that extracts a small monotone (real) circuit for clique from a short CP refutation.
- Source: [BN21], Section 7.7.2; entry as printed: "Pavel Pudlák. Lower bounds for resolution and cutting plane proofs and monotone computations. Journal of Symbolic Logic, 62(3):981-998, September 1997." Fetched URL as in B1.
- Quote [PDF text layer]: "He showed that from any short cutting planes refutation of the formula one can extract a small monotone circuit for clique, which reduces the problem to a question about size lower bounds for monotone circuits."
- Status: verified (fetched)

C2

- Source: (i) Noah Fleming, Denis Pankratov, Toniann Pitassi, Robert Robere, "Random CNFs are Hard for Cutting Planes", arXiv:1703.02469 (submitted 7 Mar 2017), fetched https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.02469 (abstract: CP refutations of random k-SAT require exponential size when k is logarithmic in the number of variables). In Proceedings of the 58th Annual IEEE Symposium on Foundations of Computer Science (FOCS '17), pages 121-131, October 2017."

D3

- Source: Svyatoslav Gryaznov, Navid Talebanfard, "Bounded-Depth Frege Lower Bounds for Random 3-CNFs via Deterministic Restrictions", arXiv:2403.02275 (v1 4 Mar 2024, v3 2 Sep 2024), fetched https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02275. [BN21] [PDF text layer]: "The only family of formulas discussed so far for which it is not known whether they possess polynomial-size Frege and/or extended resolution proofs are random k-CNF formulas; the usual conjecture is that they do not."
- Status: verified (fetched); the exact bound Ω(n^{1+ε_k}) is unverified (search snippet only), the fetched abstract reader only confirmed "super-linear".

=== E. AC0[p]-FREGE, FREGE, EXTENDED FREGE ===

E1
- Claim: No superpolynomial lower bounds are known for AC0[p]-Frege (bounded-depth Frege with mod-p gates) for any formula family, even for p = 2 (bounded-depth LK(⊕)); this is the frontier: AC0-Frege has exponential lower bounds, AC0[p]-Frege has none, despite the Razborov-Smolensky circuit lower bounds.
- Source: (i) [BN21], Section 7.10, Open Problem 7.22 (fetched URL as in B1). (ii) ECCC TR19-024, "The Surprising Power of Constant Depth Algebraic Proofs", Russell Impagliazzo, Sasank Mouli, Toniann Pitassi (20 Feb 2019; revised 11 Jan 2020), fetched https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2019/024.
- Quote: [BN21] [PDF text layer]: "Nonetheless, it is an open problem to obtain superpolynomial lower bounds for this proof system. Open Problem 7.22. Let LK(⊕) denote LK extended with a parity (exclusive or) logical connective. Are there exponential, or even superpolynomial, lower bounds on the size of bounded-depth LK(⊕) proofs?" TR19-024: "there are no significant lower bounds for AC^0[p]-Frege." and "However to date there has been no progress on AC^0[p]-Frege lower bounds."
- Status: verified (fetched). No 2024-2026 page found that reports any superpolynomial AC0[p]-Frege lower bound (see M3 for the closest 2025/2026 items).

E2
- Claim: Superpolynomial lower bounds for Frege and for extended Frege are open (EF considered harder); the survey lists as open problems finding combinatorial candidates separating Frege from EF and giving a conditional exponential Frege lower bound under an assumption weaker than NP ≠ coNP. The best known explicit Frege size lower bound is only quadratic (Ω(n^2) size, Ω(n) lines, by a subformula-counting argument attributed to Krajíček's 1995 book).
- Source: (i) Alekseev, Theory of Computing 22(4) 2026 (fetched PDF as in A1). (ii) [BN21], Section 7.9, Open Problems 7.19 and 7.20 (fetched URL as in B1). (iii) Quadratic bound: Buss, lecture notes 2002 (https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~sbuss/CourseWeb/Math267_2002W/lecture6.pdf) and problem page https://lacker.io/complexity/problems/frege-quadratic-size-barrier.html, both located by search only.
- Quote: Alekseev [PDF text layer]: "A major proof complexity problem is to prove a superpolynomial lower bound on the length of Frege proofs of arbitrary depth. A more difficult question is to prove an Extended Frege lower bound." [BN21] [PDF text layer]: "Open Problem 7.19. Find new combinatorial candidates for exponentially separating Frege and extended Frege proofs. Open Problem 7.20. Give a conditional exponential lower bound for Frege proofs using some assumption weaker than NP ≠ coNP."
- Status: verified (fetched) for openness and the two open problems; the "quadratic is the best known explicit lower bound" statement is unverified (search snippet only).

=== I. PROOF COMPLEXITY AND THE PROVABILITY OF LOWER BOUNDS ===

2. Superpolynomial lower bounds for Frege and extended Frege: open (Alekseev ToC 2026; [BN21] Open Problems 7.19, 7.20). See E2.
3. Superpolynomial lower bounds for random 3-CNFs (linear clause density) in bounded-depth Frege: open (arXiv:2403.02275 "major open problem"). See D5.
4. Cutting planes lower bounds for random k-CNF with constant k: open as of the 2021 survey ([BN21] Open Problem 7.16). See C4.
5. Superpolynomial lower bounds for general dag-like Res(⊕): open per all fetched sources through Nov 2025 (TR23-187, TR24-128, arXiv:2511.20023); a September 2026 arXiv preprint claims to resolve it for BPHP, unverified. See G6.
6. Lower bounds for SPR⁻ and PR⁻ (propagation redundancy without new variables): open per Yolcu 2024 ("pushes the frontier to ... set propagation redundancy"). See K3.
7. Automatability of Sherali-Adams: the only NP-hardness claim was retracted (TR20-064 Revision #2). See H3.
8. Whether CP with polynomially bounded coefficients (CP*) simulates general CP ([BN21] Open Problem 7.14): verified (fetched) from the chapter's open problem list.
